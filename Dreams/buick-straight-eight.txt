        <h2>The Conceptual Forensic</h2>
        <div class="forensic-text">
            <p>Every absurdity requires a catalyst. In this instance, the motivation wasn't selecting a vehicle and finding a motor—it was staring at the heavy, cast-nickel iron architecture of a 1936–1952 Buick 320 Fireball straight-eight engine and asking the immediate, unavoidable question: <em>What happens if we slap two turbos onto 34.5 inches of cast-iron inline arrogance?</em></p>
            <p>Once you commit to housing almost three feet of boost-fed iron, standard packaging goes out the window. The solution is stretching a 1939 Buick Business Coupe body directly over a custom chassis—stretching the front clip and axle line forward to create an unashamedly dramatic touring supercar silhouette. Power transfers through a modern 10-speed automatic into an independent rear suspension (IRS) setup to ensure this long-nose missile stays planted when all 700 torque-heavy horses hit the pavement.</p>
            <p>This build sits comfortably on the razor-thin boundary between genuine engineering feasibility and self-inflicted fiscal ruin. It is expensive enough, specialized enough, and mechanically stubborn enough to get pushed to the bottom of the queue indefinitely. But in the statistically improbable event of a lottery win—or should an eccentric billionaire stumble across this grid with more money than sense—this is the exact "this-because-that" blueprint to bring it to life.</p>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- TIER 3: SYSTEMIC ENGINEERING NARRATIVE -->
    <section class="content-section">
        <h2>Subsystem Solutions & Mechanical Vulnerabilities</h2>

        <!-- SECTION 1 -->
        <div class="engineering-block">
            <h3>1. Structural Reinforcement & Core Preparation</h3>
            <ul class="engineering-list">
                <li><strong>Heavy Nickel-Iron OHV Foundation:</strong> We start with the Buick 320ci Fireball OHV core because its overhead-valve cross-flow head design provides superior high-boost airflow and knock resistance compared to flathead engines, while its heavy nickel-iron block casting gives us the wall thickness needed for boost.</li>
                <li><strong>3/4-Inch Billet Main Girdle:</strong> We anchor a custom 3/4-inch billet steel girdle directly across all 5 main caps to the oil pan rail because the long, 34.5-inch block is prone to flex, and stabilizing the main caps prevents the crank from walking under 700 HP loads.</li>
                <li><strong>Deep-Tapped Deck & Ringing:</strong> We deep-tap the block deck for 1/2-inch ARP 2000 studs paired with beryllium-copper O-rings and custom MLS head gaskets because standard head bolts will stretch under high cylinder pressures, risking blown head gaskets between adjacent cylinders.</li>
                <li><strong>Priority-Main Oil Conversion:</strong> We drill internal passages to convert the block to priority-main oiling and install an external high-volume oil pump because vintage splash/feed systems cannot maintain the oil film strength required by high-load bearings and turbo oil feeds.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>

        <!-- SECTION 2 -->
        <div class="engineering-block">
            <h3>2. Rotating Assembly & Valvetrain</h3>
            <ul class="engineering-list">
                <li><strong>Dynamic Harmonic Suppression:</strong> We commission a custom billet 4340 steel crankshaft with updated counterweighting and pair it with a dual-frequency fluid damper because the long stock crank behaves like a torsional spring, and precise dampening is required to keep dangerous harmonic vibrations below 5,200 RPM.</li>
                <li><strong>Forged Steel Connecting Rods:</strong> We run custom H-beam 4340 forged steel rods rather than aluminum rods because while aluminum absorbs shock, steel provides the fatigue life and dimensional stability needed for long-term reliability on a street/strip engine.</li>
                <li><strong>Detonation-Resistant Forgings:</strong> We drop in custom forged 2618 aluminum pistons with thick ring lands and full-floating pins because cast stock pistons will shatter under detonation, while 2618 alloy withstands the heat expansion and cylinder pressures of E85 boost.</li>
                <li><strong>5,000 RPM Piston Speed Ceiling:</strong> We cap peak engine speed at 5,000 RPM because the 4.3125-inch stroke creates extreme mean piston speeds (3,593 ft/min at 5,000 RPM), meaning high power must be made through torque and boost pressure rather than revs.</li>
                <li><strong>Rigid Valvetrain & LSA Spacing:</strong> We spec thick-wall 3/8-inch pushrods and a custom billet roller cam with a wide 114°–116° Lobe Separation Angle (LSA) because thin pushrods flex under high valve spring loads, and a wide LSA minimizes valve overlap so boost isn't blown straight out the exhaust ports.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>

        <!-- SECTION 3 -->
        <div class="engineering-block">
            <h3>3. Drivetrain Adaptation & Chassis Architecture</h3>
            <ul class="engineering-list">
                <li><strong>Custom Billet 10-Speed Auto Adapter:</strong> We design a custom flywheel and bellhousing adapter plate to mate the vintage Fireball block to a modern 10-speed automatic transmission because gear-density allows us to keep the 700 HP straight-eight perpetually in its ideal powerband while offering effortless high-speed cruising.</li>
                <li><strong>Independent Rear Suspension (IRS) Module:</strong> We integrate an 8.8 IRS center section (or a custom aftermarket 9-inch IRS unit) into a multi-link rear subframe because solid axle setups compromise high-speed stability, whereas an IRS delivers modern supercar ride dynamics and anti-squat geometry.</li>
                <li><strong>Stretched Frame Rails & Proportioning:</strong> We stretch the custom chassis rails directly ahead of the firewall to accommodate the 34.5-inch engine and twin turbo plumbing, extending the front hood line of the '39 Buick Business Coupe to give it true touring supercar proportions.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>

        <!-- SECTION 4 -->
        <div class="engineering-block">
            <h3>4. Induction, Fuel & Turbo System</h3>
            <ul class="engineering-list">
                <li><strong>Equalized Plenum Manifold:</strong> We fabricate a custom sheet metal intake manifold with equal-length runners and an oversized single throttle body because stock long-runner manifolds feed cylinders unevenly, which leads to lean conditions in the end cylinders (1 and 8).</li>
                <li><strong>Sequential Port Fuel Delivery:</strong> We angle 8 individual 1,200cc+ injectors directly into the intake ports managed by a modern sequential EFI ECU because precise per-cylinder fuel control is mandatory to balance flame propagation across such a long engine block.</li>
                <li><strong>Pulse-Separated Split Manifolds:</strong> We build twin custom 4-into-1 exhaust manifolds (cylinders 1–4 on Turbo A, 5–8 on Turbo B) paired with Garrett G25-550s or BorgWarner EFR 6758s to equalize exhaust pulses and ensure instant spool off idle.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
